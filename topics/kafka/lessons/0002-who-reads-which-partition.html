Who reads which partition?

By the end, you can look at a group, its consumers and its topics, and work out who reads what, who sits idle, and what forces a reshuffle.

Lesson 1 decided where an event lands. This lesson decides who reads it.

The four rules

1. Within a group, each partition has exactly one consumer. One consumer may own many partitions, but a partition is never shared inside a group. That's what keeps a key's events in order as they're read. KafkaConsumer Javadoc
2. Partition count caps a group's parallelism. With more consumers than partitions, the extras get nothing and sit idle. To read in parallel beyond that limit, add partitions, and remember from Lesson 1 that doing so remaps keys. Confirmed on a 4.4.0 broker: 4 consumers on 3 partitions left one with 0 partitions. You'll reproduce it below.
3. Each group gets its own full copy of the stream. Groups don't affect each other, and adding a group doesn't duplicate data on the broker. Same group means share the work. Different groups means each one reads everything. KafkaConsumer Javadoc
4. Four things trigger a rebalance. A member joins. A member leaves or fails. A subscribed topic's partition count changes. A subscribed topic is created or deleted. Producers aren't on the list. KafkaConsumer Javadoc: subscribe

How the default assignor divides partitions

By default, the client's partition.assignment.strategy is RangeAssignor,CooperativeStickyAssignor, and Range is the one used. Range works one topic at a time: sort the consumers by name, divide the partitions, and give any remainder to the first consumers. Because it does this for each topic, the same consumer gets the extra partition every time. Apache's example has 2 consumers and 2 topics of 3 partitions each:

C0: t0-0, t0-1, t1-0, t1-1     (4 partitions)
C1: t0-2, t1-2                 (2 partitions)
Two newer features this lesson sets aside. The consumer rebalance protocol (group.protocol=consumer) moves assignment to the broker and doesn't use partition.assignment.strategy. In 4.4 the client default is still classic, which is what this lesson teaches. Share groups are also separate: they can have more consumers than partitions, so rule 2 applies only to ordinary consumer groups.

Try it: predict, then click

Partitions here are assigned with Range. Decide the answer before each click.

  1. billing has 2 consumers on 3 partitions. Who has the extra one, and why?
  2. Add two consumers to billing. Who ends up idle?
  3. Add a consumer to analytics. Does billing's rebalance count change?
  4. Stop the first consumer in billing. Does the idle consumer get work? Do the others keep their partitions?
  5. Click Reset, then Add topic refunds. How many partitions does each consumer have now?

Now on a real broker

Run this from the learn-kafka folder with Docker running. Start four consumers on three partitions, then look at who owns what.

source assets/kafka-lab.sh
kafka_lab_up
k kafka-topics.sh --create --topic orders \
  --partitions 3 --replication-factor 1

for c in c1 c2 c3 c4; do k_consumer_start $c billing orders; done
sleep 12
k_members billing

Now stop c1. Before running this, predict who picks up its partition.

k_consumer_stop c1
sleep 8
k_members billing

Send three events, then read them with a brand-new group. It should get all three, even though billing has already read them.

printf 'alice:o1\nbob:o2\ncarol:o3\n' | \
  k kafka-console-producer.sh --topic orders \
  --reader-property parse.key=true --reader-property key.separator=:

k kafka-console-consumer.sh --topic orders --group analytics \
  --from-beginning --timeout-ms 6000 2>/dev/null

Finally, reproduce the uneven Range split with two topics and two consumers.

k kafka-topics.sh --create --topic refunds \
  --partitions 3 --replication-factor 1
k_consumer_start a shipping 'orders|refunds'
k_consumer_start b shipping 'orders|refunds'
sleep 12
k_members shipping
kafka_lab_down

You should see c4 idle at first, then working after c1 leaves. c2 and c3 also change partitions, even though they never left. At the end, a has 4 partitions and b has 2. To see the real tool underneath k_members, run kafka-consumer-groups.sh --describe --group billing --members --verbose through k.
